Say the thing you can't say out loud.

Deep water tonight — a lot of quiet held behind cheerful faces, though a few small lights are drifting through.

Tidelight is an anonymous message-in-a-bottle for feelings. One true sentence goes into a night sea. The tide brings you a stranger's. You send back a single wordless light. That's the whole message.

The loop

Three gestures. No feed, no profile, no thread.

Drop · 140 characters

Pick the colour of the glass for how it feels, cork it, then fling it up off the screen. Claude reads it before it can exist.

Tide · the loneliest bottle first

Drag the cork out. Live people before the seeded corpus, fewest lights first, then chance. Nobody drifts unfound.

Light · hold for a second

One light. No words. Your lighthouse gets brighter, a shard of their glass lands in your jar, and you can keep the bottle as a card to share.

Beyond the light

Deeper without becoming a feed.

The Keeper · a minute with a listener

Claude, labelled as such on every screen. Not therapy. Twelve turns, nothing stored. It reflects, asks one gentle question, and names the helpline for your region the moment the water gets dark.

A note back · one way

Up to 140 characters for the writer, held to a higher bar than a bottle: warmth only, no advice, no contact details. It lands in their lantern. No thread, no reply, no one pulled into a conversation they didn't ask for.

What helped · tips, as bottles

A second kind of bottle for the small true thing that got you through. Ask the tide for one on a bad night. The ones you light are kept as driftwood on your lighthouse.

Belongs where you are

Seven languages, and a sea with your name on it.

English, Bahasa Melayu, 简体中文, தமிழ், Bahasa Indonesia, Español, 日本語. The sea's nightly line is written by Claude in the phone's language, not translated. The Keeper answers in whatever you write. The title names the nearest sea to where you are, the moon in the sky is tonight's real phase, and the Harbour knows the helpline for seventeen countries.

What isn't here

Designed by subtraction.

No streaks.

Ranks only rise. Sea glass only accumulates. Every light you send becomes a star in your sky. Gamified mental-health apps with punitive loops churn the people who need them most, so the sea never takes anything back.

No replies.

A light is the smallest possible unit of "I saw you". There is nothing to argue with and nothing to perform.

No names, no accounts.

An anonymous device id and your jar of glass. Writers can recall a bottle any time; readers can report one and it leaves the tide.

No pretending.

Seeded bottles are labelled "drift · from the sea's own stories". A real person's bottle says "live · a person, 3 min ago".

Safety on the write path

Every bottle is read by Claude before it can exist.

Three verdicts. Heavy is allowed. Names, numbers and links are held with a gentle reason. A crisis is never posted; the writer lands in the Harbour with helplines for where they are (988, Lifeline, Samaritans, Befrienders, SOS, findahelpline.com). These are real verdicts from tonight's build.

How it's built

A phone, one edge function, one model.

0audio or image assets shipped
  • The sea is drawn, not downloaded. Waves, stars, moon, bottles and the lighthouse are a SwiftUI Canvas. The cork pop, splash and chime are three WAVs generated from math.
  • The sea has a mood, and Claude writes it. Every twenty minutes Claude reads the last 24 hours of bottles and writes one sentence for newcomers, never quoting or identifying anyone. The line above is tonight's, verbatim. It tints the sky.
  • Live room. The home screen polls every six seconds, so bottles thrown from phone browsers appear within moments on the presenter's iPhone. A projector view puts the whole sea on the venue screen, glass only, never words.
  • Regex safety net. If the model is down, a keyword classifier still catches explicit crisis language and contact details. On explicit self-harm language the regex always wins.
  • Ask the tide. A small wish prefers one colour of glass, so on a bad night you can read lighter bottles, and on a good one, heavier ones that need a light.
  • On the home screen. A widget shows tonight's line and counts, refreshed every half hour, reading only public sea state.
  • Accessible. VoiceOver actions for the cork and the light, Reduce Motion slows the sea, and a launch colour so there's no white flash on a projector.
